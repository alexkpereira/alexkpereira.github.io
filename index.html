Alexander Pereira

I am a late-stage PhD Candidate in Philosophy & Psychology at Stanford University.

I mainly work in the philosophy of science—especially its overlap with mind, medicine, and mental illness. My recent research all comes back to, in one way or another, how we should understand psychiatric disorders, including how they should be explained and classified scientifically, and what a purely scientific image of mental illness leaves out.

I am a Stanford Interdisciplinary Graduate Fellow. In psychology, I am affiliated with the Stanford Psychophysiology Laboratory, which studies emotion regulation and its disorders. Before this, I studied a BSc and MPhil (Science) at the University of Sydney, advised by Dominic Murphy and Peter Godfrey-Smith. At Stanford, I work with Rosa Cao, James Gross, and Thomas Icard.

Initiatives

PRISM: Promoting the Interdisciplinary Study of Mental Health and Illness
Co-founder, with Carly Frieders. Stanford University, 2022–present.

PRISM is a reading group and research community uniting Stanford scholars from diverse disciplines to explore foundational questions about mental health and well-being. Despite decades of research, the causes and nature of mental disorders remain elusive, highlighting the need for interdisciplinary integration across sciences and humanities. Mental health is studied from medicine, cognitive science, law, policy, anthropology, and philosophy, yet no forum existed for these perspectives to meet. PRISM convenes fortnightly over dinner to discuss participant-suggested readings on questions such as: What counts as a mental disorder and who decides? How do norms shape psychiatric classification? Why has biological psychiatry struggled to deliver breakthroughs? Are AI therapists ethical and effective?
