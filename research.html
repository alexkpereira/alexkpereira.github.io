Research

Dissertation

My dissertation is a collection of papers about two undertheorized topics in the philosophy of psychiatry: plasticity and control. Plasticity, roughly, concerns the extent to which mental disorders are malleable and change over time. (I approach this with a novel synthesis of work on neuroplasticity, natural kinds, and Ian Hacking’s ‘looping effects’.) The plasticity of mental disorders has implications for longstanding debates about psychiatric classification, and about biological versus cultural explanations of mental disorder. My work on control, roughly, concerns the extent to which mental disorders can be understood as failures of personal and sub-personal regulation. (I approach this by synthesizing work on biological functions, control theory in engineering, and emotion regulation.) Understanding disorders as states of dyscontrol has deep implications for theories of psychopathology, and for the long-standing debate over whether disorder is a normative or a naturalistic concept.
